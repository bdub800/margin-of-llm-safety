# Boyd Group Services Inc. (TSX: BYD.TO / NYSE: BGSI) — Company Overview

*Prepared September 22, 2026. No stock price or price-derived metric (market cap, P/E, valuation multiple, etc.) is used anywhere in this document, per instructions.*

## 1. Business Description

Boyd Group Services Inc. ("BGSI"), through its operating company The Boyd Group Inc., is one of the largest operators of non-franchised collision repair centers in North America by both location count and sales (FY2025 MD&A, Overview). As at August 11, 2026 the company operated 1,323 collision repair locations across 41 U.S. states plus five Canadian provinces (Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, Ontario, Saskatchewan, Nova Scotia), with Canadian collision operations at 88 locations under the Boyd Autobody & Glass and Assured Automotive banners, and U.S. collision operations under the Gerber Collision & Glass banner (Q2FY26 MD&A, Overview and location table). Beyond collision repair, Boyd separately operates:

- a major U.S. retail auto-glass business under Gerber Collision & Glass, Glass America, Auto Glass Service, Auto Glass Authority and Autoglassonly.com;
- a third-party administrator, Gerber National Claims Services ("GNCS"), offering glass, emergency roadside and first-notice-of-loss services; and
- scanning and calibration operations through Mobile Auto Solutions ("MAS") in the U.S. and Volta Auto Diagnostics ("Volta") in Canada.

(Q2FY26 MD&A, Overview. The source does not separately disclose a location count for the retail glass business.)

The large majority of revenue is insurance-paid collision repair work (Q2FY26 MD&A, Overview). Boyd's business model centers on Direct Repair Program ("DRP") relationships with insurance companies, which select and retain repair shops based on average cost of repair, cycle time, customer service and quality; insurers have been consolidating DRP volume among fewer, larger operators, which the company frames as a structural tailwind for scale players like Boyd (FY2025 MD&A, Business Environment & Strategy).

**Industry context (as disclosed by the company):** the North American collision repair industry was approximately $50 billion in annual revenue in 2024, highly fragmented across roughly 30,000 repair locations; car dealerships hold about 15% of the market and multi-shop operators with more than $20 million in annual revenue hold about 42% (FY2025 MD&A, Business Environment & Strategy). With the Joe Hudson's acquisition, Boyd states it has become the second-largest independent collision repair operator in North America, though management still characterizes its own share as "modest" given the industry's fragmentation (FY2025 MD&A, Outlook).

**Growth model:** management's long-term framework (reiterated across FY2025 MD&A and the Q1/Q2 FY26 calls) targets ~3–5% average annual same-store sales growth (roughly 3–4% from total cost of repair/"TCOR" growth and ~1% from miles driven, partially offset by ~2% claims decline from collision-avoidance systems) plus 5–7% average annual growth from new locations (roughly half organic start-ups, half single-shop/small-MSO acquisitions), supplemented opportunistically by larger MSO acquisitions such as Joe Hudson's (FY2025 MD&A, Growth; Q1FY26 transcript).

Net earnings declined from FY2023 to FY2025 even as sales grew, driven by industry-wide declines in repairable claims volumes (an estimated 5–7% YoY decline in 2025, worse in earlier quarters) and by rising acquisition/transformational costs — $22.6M net-of-tax in FY2025 versus $7.3M in FY2024 and $3.2M in FY2023 (FY2025 MD&A, Selected Annual Information). Q2 FY2026 marked an inflection: quarterly sales exceeded $1 billion for the first time in company history ($1,013.7M, +29.9% YoY), Adjusted EBITDA grew 45% YoY to $135.9M (margin 13.4%, +140 bps YoY), and same-store sales turned positive for a fourth consecutive quarter (+2.9%) (Q2FY26 MD&A, Results of Operations; Q2FY26 earnings call transcript, Takeaways). Reported net earnings remain volatile/thin (Q2 FY2026: $1.3M) because of high depreciation/amortization and finance costs from rapid location and debt growth, plus a one-time $5M purchase-price-allocation amortization catch-up related to Joe Hudson's (Q2FY26 earnings call transcript).

## 2. Recent Developments (last ~12 months)

- **Leadership transition:** Tim O'Day stepped down as CEO effective May 15, 2025 and was succeeded by Brian Kaner (FY2025 MD&A, Significant Events). Steve Hoeft was appointed COO of U.S. Collision and Zach Balthrop was appointed Chief Commercial Officer, effective April 22, 2026 (Q2FY26 MD&A, Significant Events).
- **Project 360:** a company-wide cost transformation program launched Q4 2024, originally targeting $100M in annual recurring savings; ~$40M realized in 2025 via an indirect staffing model and procurement savings (FY2025 MD&A).
- **U.S. capital markets debut and financing:** BGSI completed a US$897M bought-deal equity offering in October/November 2025 — its first U.S. public offering, alongside a new NYSE listing — and issued C$275M of 5.75% notes due 2033 (September 2025) and C$525M of 5.5% notes due 2030 (November 2025), while expanding revolving credit facilities to US$675M (accordion to US$1.075B) (FY2025 MD&A, Significant Events).
- **Joe Hudson's Collision Center acquisition:** announced October 29, 2025 and closed January 9, 2026 for a transaction value of approximately $1.3 billion (Q1FY26 earnings call transcript), adding 258 collision locations across the U.S. Southeast — the largest MSO acquisition in Boyd's history, expanding the North American footprint by ~25–33% depending on the comparison base (FY2025 MD&A; Q1FY26 earnings call transcript). Combined Project 360 + Joe Hudson's synergies are now managed as a single $140M program (2025–2029); system conversion of all Joe Hudson's locations was completed by Q2 2026, ahead of schedule, allowing the 2026 synergy target to be raised twice — from $20M to $35M for Joe Hudson's alone, and total 2026 cost savings raised from $50M to $65M (Q2FY26 MD&A, Outlook and Joe Hudson's/Project 360 sections).
- **Leverage arc:** BGSI ended FY2025 in a net cash position (before lease liabilities) of $290.7M, following the equity/debt raises; after funding the Joe Hudson's purchase, net debt before lease liabilities rose to $953.1M at Q2 FY2026 ($2,003.9M including lease liabilities), with pro forma net leverage improving to 2.8x from 3.1x at year-end 2025 as EBITDA grew (Q2FY26 MD&A, Liquidity and Capital Resources; Q2FY26 earnings call transcript).
- **Capital allocation — new buyback authorization:** on September 11, 2026 the TSX approved a Normal Course Issuer Bid, which commenced September 16, 2026 and runs to September 15, 2027; management and the board stated the market price does not fully reflect the company's growth profile and cash-generating capability, positioning the NCIB as an additional capital-allocation tool alongside continued dividends and acquisitions (Knowledge Store ID 5). BGSI also declared a Q3 2026 dividend of C$0.156/share, payable October 28, 2026 (Knowledge Store ID 5) — consistent with the quarterly dividend rate maintained since Q4 2025 (Q2FY26 MD&A, Dividends).
- No other major corporate actions (M&A, litigation, regulatory) were found in a web search of company news through September 2026 beyond the above (Knowledge Store ID 6).

## 3. Key Issues Facing the Company

**(a) Total Cost of Repair (TCOR) growth remains below the long-term algorithm, and its timing is uncertain.** Management's 3–5% same-store sales framework assumes 3–4% annual TCOR growth, but TCOR growth has run persistently below that range for several quarters because elevated total-loss rates (driven by an aging, COVID-disrupted vehicle fleet and a period of weak used-vehicle pricing) have muted the mix effect (Q1FY26 earnings call transcript; Q2FY26 MD&A, Outlook). This was the single most repeated topic across every earnings call reviewed (Q4FY25, Q1FY26, Q2FY26 transcripts), with management and analysts both flagging it as the main swing factor for whether Boyd returns to its historical 3–5% same-store sales range or continues to rely almost entirely on market-share gains. Management describes this as transitory/cyclical rather than structural, pointing to a ~$2,000 cost premium on newer vehicles and continued vehicle-complexity growth (calibration penetration) as structural tailwinds once the vehicle-age mix normalizes (Q2FY26 earnings call transcript) — this is management's characterization and is not independently verified in the sources reviewed.

**(b) Execution risk and financial noise from digesting the Joe Hudson's acquisition.** At 258 locations for ~$1.3 billion, this is the largest deal in Boyd's history, executed via an accelerated ~3-month system-conversion sprint that both pulled forward cost synergies and caused temporary sales disruption and a lower-margin drag from Joe Hudson's historically thinner store economics (Q1FY26 transcript; Q2FY26 MD&A, Joe Hudson's/Project 360). It also introduced accounting noise (a $5M net earnings hit in Q2 FY2026 from a purchase-price-allocation revision) that will likely continue to distort reported (non-adjusted) net earnings for several more quarters (Q2FY26 earnings call transcript). Management reports integration is essentially complete from a systems/branding standpoint, with remaining upside from revenue synergies (cross-selling insurer relationships) still to be proven (Q2FY26 earnings call transcript).

**(c) Balance-sheet leverage has stepped up materially and its trajectory now matters.** Net debt (including leases) rose from $488M at YE2025 to roughly $2.0 billion at Q2 FY2026, and pro-forma net leverage of 2.8x (down from 3.1x at YE2025) sits inside the company's covenant ceiling of 3.50x senior funded debt/EBITDA (4.00x for four quarters post a material acquisition) (Q2FY26 MD&A, Liquidity and Capital Resources / Debt Financing). Management's growth strategy (continued tuck-in M&A plus 8–10 new start-ups per quarter) depends on this leverage continuing to decline through EBITDA growth and cash generation from a "capital-light" model (Q2FY26 earnings call transcript), which in turn depends on the TCOR/claims recovery in (a) above and successful Joe Hudson's synergy capture in (b).

**(d) A new, less capital-light lever has been added: share buybacks.** Until September 2026, Boyd's capital allocation was singularly focused on reinvestment (organic start-ups, tuck-in and large MSO M&A) funded by internally generated cash plus, for Joe Hudson's, a large debt and equity raise; as recently as May 2026 CFO Jeff Murray told an analyst that buybacks were "not in the cards in the near term" given the growth opportunity set (Q1FY26 earnings call transcript). The September 2026 NCIB authorization is a new, distinct capital-allocation option layered on top of continued dividend growth and M&A appetite, and represents a change in tone from management on how it views internally generated cash versus its acquisition pipeline (Knowledge Store ID 5). This is presented here as a factual development, not as a signal about intrinsic value, since this report deliberately excludes any price-derived analysis.
