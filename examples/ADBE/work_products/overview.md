# Adobe Inc. (ADBE) — Company Overview

*Prepared 2026-08-28. Based on Adobe's FY2025 Form 10-K, Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Q4 FY2025 / Q1 FY2026 / Q2 FY2026 earnings call transcripts (all in `primary_sources/`), and supplementary web search results saved to the Knowledge Store. Calculations in `work_products/overview_calcs.py`.*

## 1. Business summary

Adobe's stated mission is "to empower everyone to create," building "innovative platforms and tools that unleash creativity, productivity and personalized customer experiences" (Knowledge Store citation not needed — direct quote from `primary_sources/FY2025/business.md`). The company sells primarily subscription software and cloud services organized around two customer groups rather than legacy product lines:

- **Business Professionals & Consumers** — Acrobat (PDF/document productivity), Acrobat AI Assistant, Acrobat Studio, and Adobe Express (`primary_sources/FY2025/business.md`).
- **Creative & Marketing Professionals** — Creative Cloud flagship apps (Photoshop, Illustrator, Lightroom, Premiere Pro, After Effects), the Firefly family of generative AI models and apps, and the "Digital Experience" marketing stack (Adobe Experience Platform, Adobe Experience Manager, Adobe Commerce, Adobe Journey Optimizer, Adobe Marketo Engage, Adobe GenStudio) (`primary_sources/FY2025/business.md`).

Through fiscal 2025, Adobe reported three segments — Digital Media (74% of FY2025 revenue), Digital Experience (25%), and the legacy Publishing and Advertising unit (1%) — but collapsed these into a single reportable segment starting Q1 FY2026 "due to changes in how management intends to evaluate results, allocate resources and execute...strategic opportunities," reflecting a shift to unified selling motions and cross-product AI bundling (`primary_sources/FY2025/business.md`; `primary_sources/2Q2026/md_and_a.md`; segment revenue mix computed in `overview_calcs.py`).

Subscription revenue was 96% of FY2025 total revenue, underscoring the recurring, ratably-recognized nature of the business (`primary_sources/FY2025/md_and_a.md`). Geographically, the Americas contributed 59% of FY2025 revenue, EMEA 27%, and APAC 14% (`primary_sources/FY2025/md_and_a.md`). Adobe had 31,360 employees as of November 28, 2025, split roughly evenly between the U.S. and international locations, with 9.9% attrition in FY2025 (`primary_sources/FY2025/business.md`).

**First half of FY2026 (through May 29, 2026)**: total revenue was $13.02B, up 12.3% year-over-year (computed, `overview_calcs.py`, from `primary_sources/2Q2026/md_and_a.md`), with Q2 FY2026 revenue of $6.62B (+13% as reported, +11% constant currency) (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). Total Adobe ending ARR reached $27.10B, up 12.5% year-over-year as reported, including ~$480M from the Semrush acquisition closed in April 2026 (`primary_sources/2Q2026/md_and_a.md`). Since Adobe's own FY2026-opening ARR base (revalued to $25.66B), ARR has grown 5.6% through the first half — of which the organic (ex-Semrush) contribution is only ~3.7% (computed, `overview_calcs.py`), a data point relevant to the strategic shift discussed in Section 3 below.

Adobe returned significant capital to shareholders: $11.28B of buybacks in FY2025 plus $4.59B in 1H FY2026 (combined $15.87B, computed) (`primary_sources/FY2025/md_and_a.md`; `primary_sources/2Q2026/md_and_a.md`), reducing shares outstanding from 410.5 million (January 2026 10-K cover page) to 397.5 million (June 2026 10-Q cover page), a 3.2% reduction in five months (computed, `overview_calcs.py`). The board added a new $25 billion repurchase authorization in April 2026, on top of the March 2024 authorization, leaving $26.78B available as of May 29, 2026 (`primary_sources/2Q2026/md_and_a.md`).

At an approximate $290/share closing price on August 27, 2026 (Knowledge Store ID 7, web search) and ~395M shares outstanding (rough estimate extrapolating continued buybacks past the June 2026 397.5M count), Adobe's market capitalization is roughly $115B (computed, `overview_calcs.py`) — this is an approximation since I do not have a primary-source share count as of late August. Against FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $24.35–$24.45 (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`), this implies a forward P/E of roughly 12x (computed). Knowledge Store ID 8 (web search) separately describes the stock as down "22.5% year to date" and "38.0% over the past year" as of a slightly different, unspecified date, which is not fully reconcilable with the $290 print — I flag this as an inconsistency in secondhand web sources rather than a resolved fact, and would not rely on the YTD/1-year percentages without a primary-source or single-vendor price series.

## 2. Recent developments

- **CEO succession.** On March 12, 2026, Shantanu Narayen (CEO for over 18 years) announced he would transition out of the CEO role, remaining as Chair, with Lead Director Frank Calderoni leading a board search (`primary_sources/1Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). As of the June 11, 2026 Q2 call, the search was "progressing well" with a goal of having the new CEO in place "to put their stamp on planning for fiscal 2027" — implying the transition was still unresolved as of the most recent primary source available (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). A web search (Knowledge Store ID 8) indicates the search remained open into August 2026 and flags it as a source of investor uncertainty; I could not find a primary-source document in this repository confirming a successor as of today.
- **CFO transition.** On the same June 11, 2026 call, Adobe disclosed that CFO Dan Durn would depart for "a new opportunity outside the software industry," with 20-year Adobe finance veteran Steve Day named interim CFO (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). This CFO change lands in the middle of the CEO search, described directly by an analyst on the call as a "level of transition" investors would question (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`).
- **Semrush acquisition.** Adobe agreed (November 18, 2025) and closed (April 2026) the ~$1.9 billion all-cash acquisition of Semrush, a search/generative-engine-optimization platform, adding ~$480M of ARR and being integrated with Adobe Experience Manager and Adobe's "brand visibility" offering unveiled at Cannes Lions in June 2026 (`primary_sources/FY2025/md_and_a.md`; `primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`).
- **Strategic pivot toward freemium / away from near-term ARR.** Beginning with the Q2 FY2026 call, management explicitly chose to (a) route a larger share of Adobe.com traffic into free, low-friction product experiences (Acrobat, Express, Firefly) rather than direct-to-paid flows, and (b) defer previously planned Creative Cloud Pro price/line optimizations, in order to build monthly active users (MAU crossed 850M for Acrobat/Express, and Creative freemium MAU crossed 90M) ahead of monetization (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). Management quantified the near-term cost as roughly $500M of FY2026 ARR, split about evenly between the freemium redirection and the deferred price optimization (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). FY2026 total-ARR growth guidance was nonetheless raised slightly to 10.2% (from an implicit ~10% at the start of the year) because the Semrush contribution offset the deceleration (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`; `primary_sources/4Q2025/earnings_call_transcript.md`).
- **Goodwill impairment.** Adobe recorded a $70 million ($0.17/share) non-cash goodwill impairment on its Publishing & Advertising reporting unit in Q2 FY2026, alongside a legal settlement/loss contingency that also pressured G&A costs (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`; `primary_sources/2Q2026/md_and_a.md`).
- **Sell-side reaction.** Per web search (Knowledge Store ID 9), Morgan Stanley downgraded Adobe to "Underweight" on July 21, 2026, citing the freemium shift, leadership uncertainty, elevated AI investment, and AI-native competition; consensus sell-side rating is described as "Hold" (7 strong buy / 2 moderate buy / 23 hold / 2 moderate sell / 4 strong sell across 38 analysts). Adobe's next earnings report (Q3 FY2026) is scheduled for September 10, 2026 and had not occurred as of this writing (Knowledge Store ID 9).

## 3. Key issues

The following four issues are drawn from cross-referencing the FY2025 10-K risk factors, the Q2 FY2026 10-Q, and management's own commentary and analyst questions across the last three earnings calls. I have tried to keep them non-overlapping.

### 3.1 Leadership discontinuity at both CEO and CFO simultaneously

Adobe is searching for a new CEO (announced March 2026) while its CFO of nearly five years departed in June 2026 mid-search, replaced by an interim CFO (`primary_sources/1Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`; `primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). This is distinct from ordinary "key person" risk because it is a *simultaneous* dual transition at the top of the company, occurring precisely while Adobe is making a significant, self-described strategic pivot (freemium, deferred pricing, Semrush integration). Analysts on the Q2 call asked directly about "how you maintain continuity...in a world where there are a lot of questions around just disruption" (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). Narayen's answer leaned on the depth of the existing leadership bench rather than offering a timeline; as of the last primary source available (June 2026), no successor had been named. **This is a genuine, unresolved uncertainty** — the direction the next CEO sets (continuity with the freemium pivot vs. a reset) is a material unknown for the investment thesis that cannot be resolved from documents in this repository.

### 3.2 A self-inflicted, explicitly-guided near-term growth deceleration whose payback is unproven

Starting in Q2 FY2026, management chose to trade roughly $500 million of FY2026 ARR for user-acquisition metrics (MAU), by (a) sending more free-tier traffic through Acrobat/Express/Firefly rather than direct-to-paid funnels and (b) deferring Creative Cloud Pro price/packaging optimizations (`primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). This is a genuine strategic bet, not a market-driven shortfall — management repeatedly emphasized it was a choice made from a position of strength ("early success...gives us confidence to actually go even more aggressively," Shantanu Narayen, `primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). The bear case, articulated by Morgan Stanley's downgrade (Knowledge Store ID 9) and implicit in analyst questions about "payback period" on the call, is that free-to-paid conversion in AI-native creative tools is unproven at Adobe's scale and could permanently impair pricing power built over decades. My own read of the numbers: organic (ex-Semrush) H1 FY2026 ARR growth of ~3.7% (computed, `overview_calcs.py`) is running well behind the double-digit growth Adobe has historically guided to and below the FY2025 exit rate of 11.5% — management attributes the shortfall entirely to the freemium choice, but the filings do not let me independently separate "chosen deceleration" from "underlying demand deceleration." **This is the central, most uncertain judgment call in the current story.**

### 3.3 Competitive and disintermediation risk from AI-native and third-party-model creative/marketing tools

The FY2025 10-K risk factors state plainly that Adobe faces "increasing competition from companies offering generative and agentic AI solutions" and that "other companies have in the past, and may in the future prevent, limit or interfere with our ability to use third-party models in our solutions" (`primary_sources/FY2025/risk_factors.md`). This is distinct from issue 3.2 (which is about Adobe's own strategic choice) — this is about whether Adobe's moat holds as foundation-model access commoditizes generation. Management's counter-narrative is that Adobe's differentiation is commercially-safe/indemnified Firefly models, deep integration across 20+ years of workflow and customer data, and being the aggregation layer across 25+ partner models (Google, OpenAI, Black Forest Labs, Runway, Kling, etc.) rather than a single-model bet (`primary_sources/4Q2025/earnings_call_transcript.md`; `primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`). CEO commentary explicitly analogized the risk to what happened in AI coding tools ("much like how developers have embraced and expanded the AI-coding market" — `primary_sources/2Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`), framing it as both the opportunity and the threat. Note also the Q1 FY2026 disclosure that the "traditional, standalone stock book of business" (Adobe Stock) saw "a greater-than-anticipated decline," a concrete early data point of AI-generation substitution for a legacy Adobe revenue line (`primary_sources/1Q2026/earnings_call_transcript.md`).

### 3.4 Segment restructuring reduces near-term disclosure granularity precisely when investors most want to see it

Effective Q1 FY2026, Adobe collapsed Digital Media, Digital Experience, and Publishing & Advertising into a single reportable segment (`primary_sources/FY2025/business.md`; `primary_sources/2Q2026/md_and_a.md`). Management frames this as reflecting genuinely unified go-to-market motions (e.g., GenStudio and Firefly Services spanning creative and marketing). However, the effect is that investors lose the FY2025-era Digital Media vs. Digital Experience revenue/margin split at the exact moment (freemium pivot, Semrush integration, goodwill impairment in the legacy Publishing & Advertising unit) when disaggregated performance would be most informative for judging whether the ARR deceleration in 3.2 is concentrated in one part of the business or broad-based. This is an issue about disclosure/measurability, distinct from the underlying business risks in 3.1–3.3, and it constrains how confidently any external analyst — including this report — can validate management's own attribution of the ARR slowdown.

## Uncertainty summary

- **High confidence (directly sourced from filings):** FY2025 and 1H FY2026 revenue/ARR/margin figures, segment consolidation, Semrush deal terms and timing, CEO/CFO transition announcements, the freemium strategy shift and its stated ~$500M ARR cost, the Q2 FY2026 goodwill impairment.
- **Moderate confidence (web search, not cross-checked against a primary filing or a single authoritative price source):** current stock price/market cap, YTD/1-year stock performance, Morgan Stanley downgrade specifics, analyst rating distribution, CEO search status as of late August 2026.
- **Genuinely unresolved / my own inference, not fact:** whether the ARR deceleration is fully explained by the strategic choice (as management asserts) versus partly reflecting softer underlying demand; whether freemium conversion economics will work at Adobe's scale; who the next CEO will be and whether they will continue or reverse the current strategy.
