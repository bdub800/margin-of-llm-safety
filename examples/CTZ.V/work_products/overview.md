# NamSys Inc. (TSXV: CTZ) — Company Overview

*Prepared September 14, 2026. Per instructions, this overview is stock-price-blind: no current quote, market capitalization, or price-derived multiple is used anywhere below.*

## 1. Business Description

NamSys Inc. is a small Toronto-based software company that sells cloud-based, subscription-priced ("SaaS") software for managing the physical cash supply chain — retail cash handling, cash-in-transit (CIT) logistics, and bank/CIT-company cash vault processing (`primary_sources/FY2025/NamSys_MDA_022426.md`, "Overview" section). The company was originally incorporated in 1997 as Cencotech and took the NamSys name after a 2016 amalgamation; NamSys itself traces to a cash-management software business founded in 1989 (Knowledge Store ID 5; `primary_sources/2Q2026/FS_Q2_NamSys_Apr_30_26_FINAL.md`, Note 1). It lists on the TSX Venture Exchange under "CTZ" (`primary_sources/FY2025/NamSys_MDA_022426.md`).

**Products** — three interoperating product lines under the "Cirreon" and "Currency Controller" brands, per the FY2026 annual-meeting presentation (`fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`):

| Product                                                    | Function                                                                                                                            | Approx. share of revenue (FY2025)          |
|-------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Currency Controller                                        | Cash-vault processing software used by banks and CIT companies to process deposits, fulfill ATM cash orders, and reconcile clients    | ~40%                                        |
| Cirreon Retail (formerly Smart Safe & Recycler Dashboard)  | Monitoring/dashboard software for retailers' smart-safe and cash-recycler networks                                                    | ~40%                                        |
| Cirreon CIT                                                | Handheld/cloud application tracking cash pickup and delivery for armored-transport fleets                                             | ~23% (fastest-growing; +52% y/y in FY2024) |

Source for the FY2024 Cirreon CIT growth figure: 2025 annual-meeting transcript, `fetched_docs/namsys_2025_annual_meeting_transcript.md`.

**Revenue model** — almost entirely recurring subscription/maintenance revenue: 99.0% of FY2025 revenue was recurring (software subscriptions, hosted services, maintenance and product support), versus 98.2% in FY2024 and a small "professional services" residual (`primary_sources/FY2025/NamSys_MDA_022426.md`). Management frames revenue growth as ~75% organic customer growth/expansion and net-new customers over the last five years, with price increases (only introduced in the last ~2–2.5 years, tied mostly to contract renewals) contributing very little (Q&A, `fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`).

**Customers and geography** — NamSys sells to banks, cash-in-transit companies, and retailers, concentrated in the US. In FY2025, ~76% of revenue was from the US, with the balance from Mexico, Canada, the Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico and other countries (`primary_sources/FY2025/NamSys_AFS_022426.md`, Note 7). Management stated at the April 2026 annual meeting that international sales have grown to ~24% of revenue, with recent inroads in Africa and the Middle East, and that ~96% of sales are billed in US dollars even where customers are outside the US (`fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`; `primary_sources/2Q2026/FS_Q2_NamSys_Apr_30_26_FINAL.md`, Note 11). Customer concentration is moderate: three customers made up 15.9%, 12.3% and 10.7% of FY2025 revenue (`primary_sources/FY2025/NamSys_AFS_022426.md`, Note 7), and ~49% of trade receivables at FYE2025 were owed by three customers (same source, Note 3). Historically the company has experienced "no customer churn" (2025 annual-meeting transcript, `fetched_docs/namsys_2025_annual_meeting_transcript.md`).

**Headcount and operating model** — a lean, fully-remote organization: 17 employees as of the 2025 annual meeting, growing to 21 by the 2026 annual meeting, with recent hires split between development and support and aimed at extending international time-zone/language coverage (`fetched_docs/namsys_2025_annual_meeting_transcript.md`; `fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`).

**Balance sheet character** — debt-free, cash-generative, and holding a growing pile of cash and short-term GICs: total assets of $11.63M at October 31, 2025 versus total liabilities of only $0.85M, almost entirely trade payables and deferred revenue (`primary_sources/FY2025/NamSys_AFS_022426.md`, Statement of Financial Position). This is a micro-cap ($26.9M shares outstanding at FYE2025; `primary_sources/FY2025/NamSys_MDA_022426.md`) with no analyst-covered earnings calls — the closest equivalent to an earnings call transcript is the company's annual shareholder meeting, which is used as a substitute source throughout this overview.

## 4. Key Issues

The following are the most significant, and reasonably distinct, issues bearing on NamSys today, drawn from the financial filings, the FY2025/FY2026 annual-meeting Q&A (the closest available substitute for an analyst earnings call at this microcap), and recent disclosure events.

### 4.1 Founder-estate ownership transition creates a share overhang and a governance-concentration question

The company's founder and controlling shareholder for 27 years, K. Barry Sparks, died in January 2025 after owning roughly 34% of shares through his holding company Ashley Park Enterprises ULC (Knowledge Store ID 5). Through August 2026, his Estate has been distributing and selling that stake: an open-market disposition of 2,298,300 shares, plus in-kind transfers of 2,846,000 shares each to two family members (Stephanie Sparks and current Board Chair Nicole Sparks) (Knowledge Store IDs 3, 6). This is inference, not something management has directly addressed on the record in the filings reviewed: a large founding shareholder's estate settling and distributing a ~30% block is a governance event that can (a) create sustained share-supply overhang if beneficiaries continue to sell, and (b) shift effective control/influence over the company from one concentrated holder to a more dispersed set of family beneficiaries, one of whom (Nicole Sparks) already chairs the board. There is no disclosed lock-up or standstill in the sources reviewed, so the pace and extent of further estate-related selling is a genuine unknown.

### 4.2 Capital allocation: cash keeps building despite a multi-year, so-far-unsuccessful acquisition search

Management has stated for several years (2025 and 2026 annual meetings) that its primary intended use of the company's growing cash balance is acquisitions, with clearly stated screening criteria: reasonably sized (not too small, not too large), product-synergistic with the existing cash-processing focus, technologically compatible, and already profitable — explicitly "not a turnaround story" (`fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`). Despite "meaningful discussions" over multiple years, no acquisition has closed (`fetched_docs/namsys_2025_annual_meeting_transcript.md`; reaffirmed in 2026). In the interim, management has returned cash via two special dividends and ongoing NCIB buybacks (~$6.0M combined FY2024–H1FY2026, per `work_products/overview_calcs.py`), while net cash still stood at ~$0.37/diluted share at FYE2025. Management itself flagged in April 2026 that AI-driven changes to software-development economics are actively complicating this calculus — cheaper in-house build costs may make target companies look relatively less attractive to acquire versus building the same functionality internally (`fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`). This is a real, unresolved capital-allocation question: whether the company will keep returning cash as a stopgap indefinitely, or whether an accretive deal is genuinely likely — and it is not possible from the sources reviewed to handicap the odds of a deal actually closing.

### 4.3 AI is reshaping the business in ways management sees as net-positive today, but that view carries real, self-acknowledged uncertainty

Management gave an unusually detailed account of AI's effect on the business at the April 2026 annual meeting (`fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`). Key claims, all direct from that transcript and not independently verifiable from other sources: (a) AI coding tools have become standard across the engineering team, increasing "roadmap velocity" without added headcount; (b) management explicitly does **not** expect near-term margin expansion from this, because the AI-driven ability to ship more features quickly is offset by more support and onboarding burden — margin expansion is characterized as a longer-term possibility only, once new features "stabilize"; (c) management is framing the strategic response as building an interoperability "moat" (MCP/agent interfaces so Cirreon becomes a network hub other agents plug into) rather than relying on the old moat of bespoke, hard-to-replicate integrations, which management explicitly says AI is eroding for the software industry broadly; (d) management believes AI-enabled competitors and even customers building their own tools is a real, symmetric risk ("we can't ignore" it), but net assesses more advantage than disadvantage for an incumbent. All of this is management's own forward-looking assertion — it is a coherent narrative but is inherently speculative regarding whether the pricing-power and margin outcomes play out as described, and the same interview flags that pricing power industry-wide is being pressured by customers' improved ability to consider building software themselves.

### 4.4 Customer/channel concentration in the CIT segment is structurally driven by a consolidated end-market

Management disclosed at the 2026 annual meeting that roughly 75% of the US cash-in-transit market is controlled by three large players (Brinks, Loomis and Garda), and that NamSys estimates its own CIT software runs on only ~8% of all CIT vehicles in the US today (`fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`). The FY2025 MD&A separately flags "Market Condition Risks" tied to banking-sector M&A: when two of NamSys's underlying bank/CIT customers merge, the combined entity typically standardizes on one technology vendor — usually whichever had the larger installed base — which can mean losing a customer NamSys did not itself choose to lose (`primary_sources/FY2025/NamSys_MDA_022426.md`, Risk Factors). This is a structural risk distinct from ordinary customer churn (which the company says has historically been at or near zero) — it is a function of consolidation dynamics in NamSys's end-markets that NamSys does not control. Management's stated read is that this dynamic can be more opportunity than threat (a "replacement cycle" they can compete to win), but it inherently could go either way for any individual account.

### 4.5 International expansion is a stated growth pillar but remains small and unproven at scale

Management describes international sales at roughly 24% of revenue as of the 2026 annual meeting, up from a company historically ~95%+ US/North-America-weighted, with recent traction cited in Africa and the Middle East and continued build-out of support staff in the UK and Australia (`fetched_docs/namsys_2026_annual_meeting_transcript.md`). Management's own estimate of the addressable opportunity is "at least 10 times" the US market on a vehicle-count basis (same source) — an inference/estimate from management, not an independently sourced market-research figure, and explicitly caveated by management itself as a rough proxy for revenue potential rather than a revenue figure. Historically, prior attempts to expand through resellers/systems integrators were explicitly described by management as unsuccessful, because of long sales cycles and a niche vertical that resellers struggle to support (`fetched_docs/namsys_2025_annual_meeting_transcript.md`); the current strategy of hiring direct international staff is different from, and untested relative to, that earlier unsuccessful approach. The recent ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification (Knowledge Store ID 4) is a concrete, verifiable step management says supports this push, but actual conversion of the stated TAM into revenue at scale is not yet demonstrated in the financials — international revenue mix is a genuinely uncertain, still-developing part of the growth story.
